# 60 Speaking Topics — Forward Deployed Engineer track

Built for one goal: getting and doing an FDE job. An FDE spends the day explaining technical things to people who are not engineers, answering "why" questions from customers, and working inside a team. At 1 topic/day with the weekly rhythm this runs about **12 weeks**, then starts again.

Topic types:
- **EX** = Explain a technical concept. Same content for three audiences: an engineer, a non-technical manager, an executive.
- **BQ** = Behavioural interview question. Answer with a real story (STAR: Situation, Task, Action, Result).
- **WS** = Work situation. Someone at work says something; you reply as if it is real.

---

## Daily protocol

Learn first, then three runs that get shorter. Saying the same content in less time (the 4-3-2 drill) is what builds fluency.

| Step | EX | BQ / WS | Focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| Learn | read | read | The concept, what a good answer shows, the answer pattern. |
| 1 | 2:00 · to an engineer | 2:00 · cold | Content only. Don't stop. No notes. |
| — | study | study | Compare with the model answer, pick 3–5 phrases, read the better ways. |
| 2 | 1:30 · to a non-technical manager | 1:30 · with the pattern | Use an analogy (EX) / the answer pattern (BQ, WS), plus your phrases. |
| 3 | 0:45 · to an executive | 1:00 · tight version | The shortest version, then answer the follow-up question in the same run. |

Record all three. Listen back to run 3 and go through the self-check.

**Non-negotiable rule:** never restart a sentence. If you get stuck, use a filler phrase (*"let me put it another way…", "the key point is…", "good question — so…"*) and keep going. In a real meeting nobody waits for a restart.

---

## Weekly rhythm

- **Days 1–5:** new topics from the current block.
- **Day 6 — transcribe day.** Pick one recording. Write it out word for word. Mark errors yourself.
- **Day 7 — spaced revisit.** Re-do 2 topics from the *previous* block, once each, 90s: EX to a non-technical person, BQ/WS as the tight version.

Move to the next block when you've done all 10.

Typical Vietnamese-L1 patterns to watch for: missing articles, missing plural **-s**, missing past **-ed**, dropped final consonants, *he go / she have*, subject omission, and flat sentence stress. In technical talk also watch for: reading acronyms letter by letter when people say them as words, and stress on the wrong syllable in common terms (*deVELoper, ARchitecture, INtegrate, aNAlytics*).

## Self-check every 4 weeks

Re-record **Topic 221** (Tell me about yourself) each month, cold, and compare against the previous version. Measure:
- Words per minute
- Number of pauses over 2 seconds
- Number of filler sounds (uh, um)

If wpm is rising but errors aren't falling, you're getting faster at speaking badly — increase transcribe days from 1 to 2 per week.
